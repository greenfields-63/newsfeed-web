Seth’s Signals: Commentary and Its Limits

November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Commentary Is Not Reporting

“Seth’s Signals” is independent commentary. It is opinion, analysis, and interpretation written by one person. It is distinct from the headlines in the feed, which come from professional news organizations employing reporters, editors, and fact-checkers.

Commentary does not have the same obligations as reporting. It does not require multiple sources, does not require balance, and does not require neutrality. It requires a point of view. Reporting requires verification. The two should not be confused.

Separation of Functions

Commentary appears separately from headlines. It is not mixed into the news feed, it is not presented as reporting, and it is not attributed to any publisher. When commentary references a story in the feed, it links to the original publisher’s article so the reader can compare the commentary with the reporting.

This separation protects both. Commentary does not borrow the credibility of a newsroom, and newsrooms are not implicated in opinions they did not write.

Why Commentary Exists

Headlines tell you what happened. Commentary attempts to explain why it matters, what the consequences might be, and how the story fits into a larger pattern. That is a different job. It is also a more speculative one. Commentary can be wrong. Reporting can be corrected; commentary can only be revisited.

Readers should treat commentary as one person’s argument, not as verified fact. Use it to provoke thought, not to replace reporting.

Feedback and Corrections

Commentary reflects the author’s views at the time of writing. It is not updated unless the author chooses to revisit the topic. Readers who disagree or spot an error can contact the site through the Contact page.

The feed itself remains unchanged by commentary. A headline’s presence or absence in the feed is never influenced by what the commentary says about it.
