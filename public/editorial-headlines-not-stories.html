Headlines Are Not Stories

November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Compression Loses Context

A headline compresses a complex event into eight to twelve words. In that compression, nuance dies. The qualifier “allegedly” disappears. The timeframe collapses. The geography shrinks to a single city. A headline that reads “City Council Approves New Tax” does not say which tax, which neighborhoods are affected, which council members dissented, or what the revenue funds.

Readers who stop at the headline believe they know the story. They do not. They know a fragment, stripped of the conditions that make it meaningful.

The Click Is the Contract

When a publisher writes a headline, they are making a promise: click here, and you will learn more. That promise only works if the reader actually clicks. Newsfeed displays the headline and sends the reader directly to the publisher’s site. We do not host the article, we do not summarize it, and we do not insert ourselves between the reader and the reporting.

That click is also a vote. Publishers track which headlines draw traffic. When readers click through to international outlets, regional investigative desks, and non-English publications, those publishers see the traffic and know their work reached an audience. When readers never click, those outlets lose the incentive to serve that audience.

Corrections Live at the Source

Headlines are rarely corrected. Articles are. When a publisher gets a fact wrong, updates a developing story, or issues a clarification, the change appears in the full article—not in the headline that brought you there. A reader who never clicks through never sees the correction.

This matters most for breaking news, where initial reports are frequently incomplete or wrong. The first headline about a disaster, a crime, or a policy change is often revised within hours. The reader who follows the link gets the updated version. The reader who stops at the headline keeps the first impression.

What Newsfeed Does Not Do

We do not write our own versions of stories. We do not generate AI summaries of full articles. We do not place advertisements between the reader and the publisher’s content. We display headlines, provide translation when requested, and link out. The reporting belongs to the publishers. The reader’s attention belongs to the publishers too.

If you value the journalism you find through Newsfeed, support the organizations that produce it. Subscribe, donate, or at minimum, turn off your ad blocker on their sites. Headlines without newsrooms behind them are just words.
