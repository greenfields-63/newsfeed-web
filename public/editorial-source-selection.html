How We Choose Sources

November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Geographic Coverage

Newsfeed selects sources from thirty-three outlets across thirteen countries: Argentina, Australia, China, France, India, Israel, Japan, Lebanon, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Spain, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The goal is geographic spread, not partisan balance within any single country.

A reader in the United States can see how the same event is reported in Buenos Aires, Beijing, London, and Tokyo. A reader in Israel can compare coverage from Ynet, Jerusalem Post, and Haaretz alongside BBC, Al Arabiya, and Reuters. The mix is deliberately international.

Public Access

Every source in the feed offers a publicly accessible RSS feed. We do not include outlets behind paywalls, login walls, or geographic blocks that prevent readers from clicking through to the full article. If a reader cannot reach the original story, the headline is less useful.

This requirement excludes some prominent outlets, but it keeps the feed honest: every headline links to a story anyone can read.

Update Frequency

Sources are checked every ten minutes. Outlets that publish frequently—wire services, broadcasters, digital-native newsrooms—appear more often in the feed simply because they produce more headlines. Outlets that publish less frequently appear less often. Neither frequency nor prominence determines inclusion; availability does.

Readers can follow or unfollow any source. The feed respects those choices immediately.

No Editorial Vetting of Content

Inclusion in the feed is not an endorsement. Newsfeed does not fact-check individual headlines, does not verify the accuracy of every story, and does not remove a source because its editorial line disagrees with ours. A source remains in the feed as long as it publishes publicly and updates regularly.

Readers are responsible for evaluating the credibility of each source. That is why source names and countries are displayed with every headline.
